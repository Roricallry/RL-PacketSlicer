**ai**: Let me read the note first to see what it already covers.“多臂”（duō bì）comes from the classic **multi-armed bandit** (MAB) problem, and the metaphor is a row of slot machines: “arm” = **摇杆/拉杆** (the lever you pull), and “多臂” means there are **many levers to choose from**. So “上下文**多臂**赌博机” is just “Contextual Bandit” written out in full — 多臂 isn't a new concept on top of it, it's describing the action space as “choose one of k levers.”

### Where it comes from

Picture a casino with $k$ slot machines (in Chinese they're often 老虎机). Each machine has its own unknown payout distribution. Every round you pull **one** lever and receive a reward, and you want to maximize total reward over $T$ rounds. You do **not** know the payoffs; you only learn them by pulling.

The dilemma is the classic **探索与利用 (exploration vs. exploitation)** trade-off:
- **利用 (exploit)**: keep pulling the arm that looked best so far.
- **探索 (explore)**: pull a less-tried arm in case it's actually better. A pull with nothing new learned is wasted, and a pull that gives no reward may prevent you from earning more, so you have to balance the two.

Formally: arms $a \in \{1,\dots,k\}$, each with unknown mean reward $\mu_a$. The regret after $T$ rounds is

$$R_T = T\mu^* - \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mu_{a_t}, \quad \mu^* = \max_a \mu_a$$

Good algorithms (UCB, Thompson Sampling, $\varepsilon$-greedy) achieve sublinear regret, roughly $O(\sqrt{kT\log T})$.

### The “上下文” part

In plain MAB, the best arm is fixed for everyone and every situation. **Contextual** bandit adds a context vector $x_t$ before each round: you observe the situation first, then pick an arm based on it. So the payoff is $\mu(a \mid x_t)$, and the optimal arm **changes with the context**.

Example: an ad system. Context = user features (age, device, time of day); arms = which ad to show. The best ad depends on who's looking. A fixed “always show ad 3” is the plain MAB answer; “look at the user, then decide” is the contextual one. LinUCB works because it assumes the reward is linear in the context, $\mu(a\mid x)=\theta_a^\top x$, and estimates $\\theta_a$ per arm with ridge regression.

### So how do the terms stack up?

- **Bandit (赌博机)**: reward only for the action you chose, no feedback on the others. This is the key break from supervised learning.
- **Multi-armed (多臂)**: $k > 1$ arms, so choosing between arms is the whole problem. With $k=1$ there'd be nothing to decide.
- **Contextual (上下文)**: a feature vector arrives each round and the best arm depends on it.
- Plain MAB is the special case of contextual bandit where the context carries no information.
